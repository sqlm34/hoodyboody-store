01 — The Problem

Why Knit Fabric Needs Special Stabilization

Knit fabrics such as jersey, fleece, pique, and interlock are constructed with interlocking loops rather than woven threads. This gives them their characteristic stretch and makes them notoriously difficult to embroider. Without proper stabilization, three things happen:

Puckering

The fabric gets pulled and gathered around the design, creating ripples that will not iron out.

Design Distortion

Letters and shapes stretch out of proportion as the fabric moves under the needle.

Thread Breaks

The fabric movement creates tension spikes that snap threads mid-stitch.

The right stabilizer acts as a temporary rigid backing, giving the needle a stable surface to punch through while keeping the fabric from shifting or stretching during the embroidery process.

02 — Stabilizer Types

The 3 Stabilizer Types Explained

Cut-Away Stabilizer

Best for knit fabrics. Cut-away is a permanent stabilizer that remains in the garment after embroidery. You trim the excess close to the design edges, and it continues to support the stitches through every wash.

  • Stays in garment permanently
  • Available in light, medium, and heavy weight
  • Available in black or white
  • Must be trimmed after stitching

Tear-Away Stabilizer

Not ideal for knits. Tear-away is removed after stitching by tearing it away from the design. While convenient on stable woven fabrics, the tearing motion can distort delicate knit stitches.

  • Removed after stitching
  • Fine for stable woven fabrics
  • Avoid on high-stretch knits
  • Can be used for very low stitch count designs

Water-Soluble Topping

Use on top of knits. Water-soluble stabilizer such as Solvy or Badgemaster is placed on top of the fabric, not beneath it. It prevents stitches from sinking into textures and dissolves completely in water.

  • Goes on top of the fabric
  • Essential for fleece and polo shirts
  • Dissolves in warm water
  • Use in combination with cut-away
The Golden Rule for Knits Use cut-away stabilizer beneath plus water-soluble topping above. This combination handles 90% of knit fabric embroidery situations perfectly.

05 — How-To

How to Apply Stabilizer on Knit Fabric

Cut the Stabilizer Larger Than the Hoop

Cut your cut-away stabilizer 2 to 3 inches larger than the hoop on all sides. This prevents the hoop from cutting into the stabilizer and losing support at the edges.

Hoop Only the Stabilizer

Place the stabilizer in the hoop tight like a drum. Never stretch it. The stabilizer alone goes in the hoop; the garment is attached separately.

Spray Adhesive on the Stabilizer

Spray a light, even coat of temporary adhesive onto the hooped stabilizer. Wait 10 seconds for it to become tacky.

Lay the Garment Flat and Position

Lay the garment smoothly onto the adhesive stabilizer. Use a placement template or center marks to position the design area precisely. Smooth out any wrinkles from the center outward.

Add Water-Soluble Topping if Needed

For polo shirts, fleece, or any textured knit, lay a piece of water-soluble stabilizer on top of the fabric and pin or tape at the corners to hold it in place.

Stitch, Remove, and Trim

Embroider the design. Remove the water-soluble topping by rinsing or peeling away. Trim the cut-away stabilizer from the back, leaving a quarter inch around the design perimeter.

Common Mistake Never pull the t-shirt fabric taut when positioning it on the stabilizer. Applying the shirt while stretched means it will pucker when relaxed after embroidery. Always position the garment completely relaxed and flat.
06 — FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use tear-away stabilizer on a t-shirt?

Generally no. The stretchy nature of jersey fabric means tear-away does not provide enough support, and the tearing motion can distort the stitches. Cut-away is always the safer choice for knits.

Do I need topping on every t-shirt?

On smooth cotton t-shirts with a clean surface, topping is optional. On polo pique, fleece, waffle knit, or any textured fabric, water-soluble topping is essential to prevent stitches from sinking in.

How many layers of stabilizer do I need?

For most t-shirts, one layer of medium-weight cut-away is sufficient. For high-stretch fabrics such as Lycra or activewear, or very dense designs, use two layers of light cut-away for extra control.

Can I wash out the stabilizer?

Cut-away stabilizer stays in the garment permanently. It does not wash out. Only water-soluble stabilizer, used as topping, dissolves in water. This is by design: the cut-away continues to support your stitches through future washes.
